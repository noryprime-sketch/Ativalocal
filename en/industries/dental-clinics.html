How patients choose a dental clinic

Choosing a dentist is a matter of trust. Someone in pain searches "dentist near me" and wants to know if they can be seen quickly. Someone thinking about an implant or braces compares several clinics, reads reviews, looks at who the dentists are and tries to understand how the treatment goes before booking a first appointment.

Many searches start with a specific question: whether the clinic works with their insurance, offers clear aligners, sees children or has parking nearby. When the website doesn't answer any of this, the patient calls another clinic or puts off the decision.

A well-organised website gives the main specialties their own page, which helps Google show the clinic in specific searches, and presents the team with names and licence numbers. All in informative language, with no promises of results, as Portuguese law requires for healthcare advertising.

Healthcare advertising within the rules

Informative text, no promises of results, and clinic identification with its ERS registration, as required by Decree-Law no. 238/2015 and the ERS Regulation.

Forms without clinical data

The form asks only for name, contact details and preferred time. Health data is sensitive under the GDPR and is left for the appointment, outside the website.

Frequently asked questions

Questions about dental clinic websites

What do I have to show on the website as a healthcare clinic?

ERS Regulation no. 1058/2016, which complements Decree-Law no. 238/2015, requires healthcare advertising to identify who is behind it: name or company name, tax number (NIF), ERS registration number and operating licence number, where applicable. On the website, these details go in the footer of every page, as well as the identification of the dentists.

Can I put prices and promotions on the website?

You can show prices, as long as they are clear, truthful and say what's included. Portuguese healthcare advertising law bans misleading messages, such as promising guaranteed results or hiding the conditions of an offer. That's why we keep the text informative and the clinical director approves everything before launch.

Is it safe to receive appointment requests through the website?

Yes, if the form asks only for what's needed: name, contact details and preferred time. We tell patients not to describe symptoms or medical history, because health data is a special category under the GDPR and should be handled at the appointment, in the clinic's own systems. The website has a privacy policy and a secure connection.

Should I publish the team and their licence numbers?

We recommend it. Seeing names, photos and areas of work builds trust before the first appointment, and the licence number lets patients check each dentist's registration with the Portuguese Dental Association. Details and photos are only published with each professional's permission.

Can I use before-and-after photos?

It's a sensitive area. Before-and-after images can be read as a promise of results, which healthcare advertising law doesn't allow. If you want to use them, you need the patient's written consent, real unretouched cases and a note saying results vary. If in doubt, check with the ERS or the OMD before publishing.

Does the website help me show up when people search for a dentist in my area?

It helps, but nobody can guarantee rankings on Google. Local SEO is a separate service that can be included in the proposal: specialty pages with the name of the town, the same clinic details everywhere and a well-completed Google Business Profile. Patient reviews on Google also count, and the website can include a simple link for leaving them.
