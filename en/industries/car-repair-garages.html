How drivers choose a garage

People who need a mechanic are usually in a hurry. A strange noise, a warning light on the dashboard or an upcoming inspection lead to searches like "mechanic near me" or "garage" followed by the name of the town. The choice is made on the phone, among the garages on Google Maps, based on reviews, distance and opening hours.

Then comes the biggest question: how much will it cost. Many drivers don't like leaving their car without an idea of the price. A garage that shows its services, gives guide prices for common jobs and replies quickly to a quote request has an edge over those with just a phone number.

Your own website gives that clarity. It explains what the garage does, which makes it works on, whether it handles tyres, air conditioning or electronic diagnostics, and lets customers send the registration number and a description of the problem on WhatsApp. The garage gets the request with the information it needs to reply.

Frequently asked questions

Questions about garage websites

Should I show prices on my garage website?

Showing guide prices for common jobs, such as an oil and filter change or brake pads, helps customers decide and avoids calls just to ask about prices. Just say "from" and explain that the final price depends on the make, the model and the condition of the parts. Bigger jobs are always subject to a quote.

How does the WhatsApp quote request work?

The customer taps the button and WhatsApp opens with a ready-made message asking for the registration number, model, mileage and a description of the problem. They can add photos or a video of the noise. The garage gets everything on the phone and replies when it has a moment, without taking calls in the middle of a job.

Does the website show up when someone searches "mechanic near me"?

Those searches mainly show Google Maps, which takes into account distance, reviews and the information in the Business Profile. The website strengthens that profile with well-described services and location. Work on the profile and local SEO are a separate service. Nobody can guarantee a specific position on Google, and you should be wary of anyone who does.

Is a page about the MOT-style periodic inspection worth having?

Yes, because many people look for somewhere to check their car before the inspection. The inspection is done at inspection centres, not at the garage, but the page explains what's usually checked and what the garage can look at beforehand. If the car fails, the garage also handles the repairs for the re-inspection.

I already have a Google listing and a Facebook page. Do I need a website?

The Google listing is essential and we can improve it as a separate service, but it has little room to explain services, makes and prices. The website completes that information and is where the listing points to. Facebook is good for news, but posts get lost over time and don't answer the questions of people looking for a garage.

Can I say on the website that I work on a specific make?

You can list the makes you work on or have experience with, as long as it's clear you're an independent garage if you're not a dealer or authorised repairer. Brand logos may require permission, so we prefer the name in text. It helps people looking for a mechanic with experience of a particular make in your area.
